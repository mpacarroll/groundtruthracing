Mexico City qualifying: why the pole call missed

  1. The margin was inside the noise. From their best FP3 lap to their best qualifying lap, the top six found between 0.87 s (Norris) and 1.58 s (Russell); across the top ten the spread was 0.25 s (one standard deviation). A 0.10 s FP3 edge is a coin toss, and the read should have said "six cars live for pole", not a name.
  2. It was corners, not power. Against Hamilton's pole lap, by corner class: Antonelli was 0.18 s up on the straights and 0.37 s down in the corners (0.21 in slow ones); Russell was 0.12 s up on the straights and 0.17 s down in slow corners, 0.009 s off. Speed traps were level between FP3 and qualifying (Hamilton 348 and 347 km/h), so there was no engine-mode reveal.
  3. Our own data pointed the other way. The 2026 team table (out/season/2026/team_classes_median.csv) has Ferrari best in medium and slow corners and Mercedes best on straights. Mexico's lap is 41% corners under 210 km/h by road length (1.72 of 4.23 km). The FP3 read used lap time only and never crossed the team table with the track's mix.
  4. What held: the pole time range, and the Mercedes straight-line edge.

Mexico City race: half right

  1. Pace, two of three. Verstappen and Leclerc were the two fastest cars in the race, 0.03 s apart on median green lap, which is what the FP2 long runs said. Norris was not: 0.55 s off and P5. His FP2 run was on softs with a flat slope; the race did not repeat it.
  2. Wear, wrong. FP2 long runs lost 0.06 to 0.12 s a lap per lap of age; the race lost 0.011 (soft) and 0.013 (medium), the same as 2025. Friday long runs carry heavy fuel and an unrubbered track, so their slope is fuel plus evolution, not wear. A Friday slope is not a race wear forecast.
  3. Strategy open, right for the wrong reason. Nobody one-stopped (ten did in 2025) and the field split between three and four sets, but not because the tyres wore out: the medians show they held. The safety-car period mid-race gave everyone a cheap stop.
  4. Leclerc. He topped FP1 and FP2, was P3 in FP3, qualified P3, and had the second-fastest race car. Across all five sessions he was the most consistent signal of the weekend, and neither read named him first. The pole read used FP3 alone and the race read hedged across three names. He did not win either session, so naming him would also have missed, but a read that weighs all three practice sessions puts him at the top of the list, and the method should.

The model, 2026-11-02

The question: which drivers and cars like fast corners, and which like slow ones, and does knowing that predict qualifying? The model:

  • Inputs, all measured: each driver's best qualifying lap at each 2026 track, split by corner class on the track's micro sectors (out/season/2026/driver_classes.csv, 315 laps at 16 tracks), and each track's kilometers of each class.
  • Profile: seconds per km behind the best driver in that class, median across tracks. Car is the team median; driver style is the gap to the teammate per km.
  • Prediction: profile times the next track's kilometers per class, summed.
  • Backtest, walk-forward from round 5: pole called 6 of 12 (season-average gap with no classes: 2 of 12; last round's order: 3 of 12); top-3 overlap 1.9 of 3 against 1.7 and 1.3; rank correlation 0.92 for all three, so the classes help at the front, not down the order. At Mexico it picked Leclerc, Antonelli, Hamilton.
  • What it is not: no simulation and no invented parameters; the real-data rule stands. It is medians of measured gaps.

Method changes

  • A pre-quali read names every driver within the FP3-to-qualifying noise band (about two standard deviations, 0.5 s, of the top ten's gains at this track or the last), not one name.
  • It crosses each team's season strength by corner class with the circuit's class mix, and says when that disagrees with the FP3 order.
  • It says what changed between FP3 and qualifying in past years (speed traps, class gains) before reading FP3 as qualifying pace.
  • A pre-quali read weighs all three practice sessions, not FP3 alone, and says who was consistent across them.
  • A Friday long-run slope is reported as "fuel plus evolution plus wear", never as wear, and the race read's wear call comes from last year's race at the circuit instead.

Every call is logged before the session and graded after it, misses included.
